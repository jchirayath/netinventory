Every device on your network, named and in its room.

netinventory finds everything on your home network, from the TV to the leak sensor in the garage. It keeps the names you give them, tells you when something new joins or a device you care about drops off, and warns you before a battery dies.

Get it on GitHub See what it does

Know what is on your network

Routers forget names and hand out new addresses. netinventory keys every device by its hardware address, so a name you give it sticks, and it brings together what the router, the network itself and your smart-home apps know about each one.

One list of everything

Every device with its address, vendor, signal strength and when it was last seen. A device with wired and Wi-Fi connections is one row, and anything you haven't named yet is easy to find.

  • Found through the router, a network sweep, SSDP and mDNS
  • Smart-home clouds fill in what each app calls a device
  • Unknown devices get a vendor lookup and a fingerprint

Every device in its room

The rooms board shows the house the way you think about it. Drag a device to another room, type or app. Each device gets one room and one name, and netinventory can rename it to match in Home Assistant, SmartThings, Google Home, Alexa, Apple Home and Smart Life.

Batteries, before they die

Levels from your sensors, locks and cameras, worst first. Log a battery change with its type, and netinventory notices replacements on its own, learns how long a set lasts and tells you when the next change is due.

Click a device for its level over time and every change.

Alerts that matter

An email or notification when a new device joins, an address changes, or a device that was gone for a month comes back.

Watch the ones you care about

Pick devices to monitor. You hear once when one stays offline or its battery runs low, and once when it recovers.

A companion app

The same devices and batteries on your Mac and iPhone, with edits that need your passcode.

Your smart-home apps fill in the rest

A network scan sees an address and a vendor. Your apps know what you named each device, its model, its battery, and whether it's really online, even for sensors that never touch Wi-Fi. netinventory signs in to each app with your own account and puts that detail on the same row.

Home Assistant

Every battery from any integration it runs, and names. It can also set names and areas back.

Blink

Each camera and doorbell: model, battery, signal, whether it and its sync module are online, and when it last checked in.

Flume

The water sensor and its bridge: battery level and whether each is connected.

Tuya and Smart Life

App names, addresses, battery and online state. It can file devices into their rooms.

Govee

App names and models, plus battery and online state for leak sensors behind a hub.

SmartThings

Device labels and network addresses. It can file devices into their rooms.

Wyze, Kasa and Apple

Names, models and addresses, so a device shows the name you already gave it.

Bluetooth

Battery and presence of thermometers your Mac can hear, with no account at all.

Sign-ins stay in the macOS Keychain, or an owner-only file on Linux. Battery levels are read every hour and kept as a history.

Runs at home, reachable anywhere

A Mac does the scanning, because it has the radios and the CPU. A Raspberry Pi serves the dashboard to the house all day. A copy on the web lets you check from anywhere, and the Pi keeps it current over one outbound connection, so nothing on the internet ever connects into your home.

One machine is enough to start: run it on your Mac or your Pi alone, and add the others later.

Get started

You need Python 3.8 or later. The core needs nothing else; cloud connectors and Excel reports are an optional install.

  1. Get the code.

    git clone https://github.com/jchirayath/netinventory
cd netinventory/engine

  2. Scan your network and build the inventory.

    python3 netinv.py update --scan

  3. Open the dashboard at http://127.0.0.1:8787.

    python3 netinv.py serve

  4. To see it on your phone and edit from other devices, set a passcode and share it on your network.

    python3 netinv.py set-passcode
python3 netinv.py serve --lan --allow-write

The engine guide covers cloud connectors, alerts, monitors and running on a Raspberry Pi.

Your network stays yours

netinventory runs on your own machines. There's no account and no service to send your data to.

  • Read-only unless you say so. Editing is off until you turn it on, and other devices need your passcode.
  • No plain-text passcode. It's stored only as a salted hash, and repeated wrong guesses lock that device out.
  • Credentials in the Keychain. Logins for your smart-home apps stay in the macOS Keychain, or an owner-only file on Linux.
  • A record of every edit. Who changed what, and every wrong passcode, kept on the machine where it happened.
